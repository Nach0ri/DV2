Things to keep in mind

  • More rescues does not mean an area is more dangerous for wildlife. The numbers also depend on how many people live there, how much bushland there is, and whether a rescue group is nearby.
  • The map shows the number of rescues, not a rate. Dividing by population or land area would measure something different, so I have left the counts as they are.
  • The colour groups stay the same for every year, so you can compare years fairly.

Data sources

Rescue numbers come from the NSW Wildlife Rehabilitation Data Dashboard (NSW Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water). They cover native birds, mammals, reptiles and amphibians rescued by licensed groups from 2013–14 to 2024–25. The dashboard was last updated on 26 May 2026 and the numbers were downloaded on 15 October 2026.

These numbers do not match the NSW Wildlife Rehabilitation Annual Report 2023–25. For example, the report says Tweed had 6,080 rescues in 2023–24, but the dashboard shows 3,155. The report's state totals (122,258 and 145,110) are also higher than the dashboard's (107,202 and 119,646). The two seem to use different versions of the data, so the numbers on this page are dashboard numbers.

Council boundaries are from NSW Spatial Services (132 local government areas).

About this chart

The reason categories come from the NSW record sheet for fauna rehabilitators (the “Encounter Type” sheet in the 2026–27 template). Each rescue is recorded with one reason.

Bars are sorted from most to least rescues for the chosen year. The file does not include the “unknown” reason, which the NSW report says covers 49% of 2022–23 rescues, so the bars do not add up to all rescues and no share is shown.

Figures match the NSW Wildlife Rehabilitation Annual Report 2022–23 (reasons for rescue, PDF page 15).

When do wildlife rescues peak?

What the data shows

  • Birds peak in spring and early summer. November is the highest month for birds on average. Across all rescues, September to November made up 31–35% of each year.
  • Reptiles peak in January on average (about 13% of their rescues), and are lowest in July.
  • Mammals do not have a stable peak. The highest month changes from year to year (September, October, February, January and August across the six years), so no single season stands out.

Data source and limitations

Data come from supplementary file S7 (pone.0257209.s007.xlsx) of Kwok et al. (2021), PLoS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0257209. They cover financial years 2013–14 to 2018–19 and are historical. They are not current NSW statistics.

Animal groups. The source reports 42 sub-groups. They are grouped as follows: Birds = cuckoos, diurnal birds of prey, emu, herons and allies, honeyeaters, kingfishers, marine birds, other aquatic birds, other birds, owls/nightjars/frogmouths, parrots, passerines, pigeons and doves; Mammals = bandicoots/bettongs/potoroos, echidna, feathertail glider, flying-foxes, koala, large gliders, large kangaroos, marsupial dasyurid, microbats, platypus, possums, rodents, small macropods, unknown macropod, unknown mammals, wombat; Reptiles = bearded dragons and blue tongues, freshwater turtle, geckoes, large reptiles and monitors, small skinks and lizards, snakes (others and pythons), unidentified turtle/tortoise.

Counts are sums of sub-groups, so groups do not overlap. The source's “Not recorded” rows (no month) are left out. The “Average” option is the mean of each year’s monthly count, not a combined total. Monthly values are rescue counts, not rates, so differences in population or search effort are not adjusted for.

Which threats peak when?

What the heatmap shows

  • Abandoned or orphaned animals peak from October to December (about 6,100–7,700 rescues a month across all groups), mostly birds. The count is lowest in mid-year.
  • Vehicle collisions are highest from July to October, peaking in August at 5,248 rescues. Mammals drive most of the July–September rise.
  • Do not read this as complete seasonality. “Unknown” causes make up 54% of all rescues, and they are not shown here. The source does not give years, so the pattern is for the whole period combined.

Data source and limitations

Data come from supplementary file S8 (pone.0257209.s008.xlsx) of Kwok et al. (2021), PLoS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0257209. The data are historical and are not current NSW statistics.

Cleaned data: data/rescue_reasons_by_month.csv (animal group, reason, month, count). Each row is one cause for one animal group in one month. The “total” rows in the source are left out so nothing is counted twice, and the group rows were checked against those totals for every month. “All classes” adds the three groups together.

Left out: the “Unknown” cause (150,015 + 73,120 + 29,383 = 252,518 rescues), and the “No month reported” column. Blank cells in the source are treated as not reported, not zero.
